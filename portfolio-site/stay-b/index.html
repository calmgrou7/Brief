Before I arrive

Documents to bring

Keep these in your hand luggage, not in your suitcase.

  • Passport, with enough validity left (to be confirmed)
  • Your visa and your school's admission letter
  • Copies of your academic certificates
  • A few passport-size photos (to be confirmed)
  • Some Korean won in cash for your first days

Source: Your school's international office · Korea Immigration Service

Finding a safe place to live

Before paying a deposit, check your housing options with your school.

  • Ask your school about dormitories first.
  • Never send a deposit before you see a written contract.
  • Check that the person you pay is the real owner or a registered real estate agent.
  • Keep every receipt and message.

A housing list will be shared at our info sessions.

Source: Your school's international office

Official guidance
Your first day in Korea

Landing, getting a SIM, and getting to your campus.

  • Get a prepaid SIM at the airport with your passport, so you can use maps and messengers.
  • Buy a transport card for buses and the subway.
  • Plan how you will get to your campus before you land (train, bus or domestic flight).
  • Save your school's and dormitory's address in Korean.

Source: Korea Tourism Organization

Just arrived

Alien registration: what to bring

Check the requirements for your stay before booking a visit.

  • Use Hi Korea to find the office for your home address.
  • Confirm the application deadline, documents and fee with the office or 1345.

Source: Hi Korea

Official guidance
Bank and phone: the right order

Some steps need your alien registration card first. This order saves trips.

  1. Prepaid SIM with your passport (day 1)
  2. Alien registration (book and visit)
  3. Bank account with your card and Korean phone number (to be confirmed)
  4. Switch to a monthly phone plan if you want (to be confirmed)

Source: Financial Supervisory Service · your bank

Health insurance

Check how health insurance applies to your stay.

  • Use the NHIS foreign resident guide to check enrollment and contributions.
  • Ask your international office whether your school offers additional cover.

Source: National Health Insurance Service

Official guidance
Getting around and emergencies

Save these numbers today.

  • 112 Police · 119 Fire and ambulance
  • 1345 Immigration Contact Center, with foreign language help (to be confirmed)
  • Map apps that work well in Korea: Naver Map, KakaoMap
  • One transport card works on Busan buses and subway.

Source: Korea Immigration Service · Busan Metropolitan City

Study, life & work

Part-time work: the basic rules

You need permission before your first day of work. Not after.

  • Get part-time work permission from immigration first, with your school's confirmation (to be confirmed)
  • Allowed hours depend on your visa, Korean level and whether it is semester or vacation (to be confirmed)
  • Some kinds of work are not allowed for students (to be confirmed)
  • Working without permission, or too many hours, can put your visa at risk.

Source: HiKorea · your school's international office

Official guidance
Before your first job: a checklist

Five things to check before you say yes.

  • Your work permission is approved.
  • You have a written work contract.
  • Your pay is at least the minimum wage (to be confirmed)
  • You know your pay day and get a pay slip.
  • Never hand over your passport or alien registration card to an employer.

Stay B shares general job information. Job introductions are made by registered employment agencies and job centers.

Source: Ministry of Employment and Labor

Official guidance
Saving on everyday costs

Small savings add up over a semester.

  • Use student discounts on transport where available (to be confirmed)
  • Cook with friends and shop at traditional markets.
  • Show your Stay B membership card at partner shops for discounts.
  • Check your school for free events and meals.

Source: Busan Metropolitan City

When you feel alone

Feeling homesick is normal. You do not have to handle it alone.

  • Your school has a counseling center. Ask the international office.
  • Join students from your country at our meetups.
  • If you are in danger, call 112 or 119.

Source: Your school

Visa & after graduation

Know your dates

Use the date on your residence document to count the days remaining.

  • Check the expiry date on your current residence record.
  • Use Hi Korea to check the procedure for your status, or ask 1345.

Source: Hi Korea

Official guidance
Extension or passport renewal?

Check which office handles each document.

  • For a stay extension, contact Korean immigration.
  • For passport renewal, contact the embassy or consulate of your country.
  • After renewal, ask immigration what changes you need to report.

Source: Korea Immigration Service

Official guidance
Staying after graduation

Plan your next step with your university and immigration office.

  • Read the official information on residency and job-seeking after graduation.
  • Ask your international office which documents your school can issue.

Source: Study in Korea

Official guidance
Big life changes

Family visits and changes in your circumstances can involve different procedures.

  • Before planning a family visit or a change of status, contact immigration or your consulate.
  • Have your question and current status ready when contacting 1345.

Source: Korea Immigration Service

How we work

Who can help?

So you always know who is helping you, and nobody asks you to pay for something they are not allowed to do.

Stay B helps with

  • Guides and checklists based on public information
  • Info sessions, settling-in help, welcome kit, discounts and meetups
  • Community chats and expiry date reminders
  • Passing your question to a partner agent, only with your consent

A qualified administrative agent (Haengjeongsa) handles

  • Advice on your own case
  • Preparing and filing visa and stay documents
  • Any fee for this work, agreed directly between you and the agent
  • Stay B does not charge any fee for administrative work and does not take a share of the agent's fee.
  • Job introductions are made by registered employment agencies and job centers.
  • Stay B does not offer matchmaking or marriage brokering.
  • Stay B does not recruit students, handle school admissions, or apply for visas.

Your information is used only to help you, and shared with an agent only when you agree. Never send your passport or alien registration number.

Common questions

Is Stay B free?

The guides on this site are free to read, and asking us a question is free. Optional in-person services, like airport pickup, will be paid services. Details and prices are coming soon.

Is Stay B a government office or an administrative agent?

Neither. Stay B is a private information, support and community service for international students. See How we work.

Can you tell me if my visa will be approved?

No. We share general information, and connect you to a qualified partner agent who can look at your case.

Where can I meet you in person?

In Busan, and before you arrive, at info sessions with our partner language school in Nepal. Places and dates will be shared after partner talks.

Can you find me a part-time job?

We explain the rules and share general job information. Job introductions are made by registered agencies.

I am getting married in Korea. Can you help?

If your marriage is already decided, we share general information on the stay steps that follow, and connect you to a partner agent. We do not offer matchmaking or marriage brokering.

Which languages do you support?

English and Korean first, then Nepali. More languages may come as we grow.

For universities, language schools and partners

Stay B provides general information based on public sources, offline settling-in support, and a student community and consultation intake. It is not a government agency or an administrative agent's office. Stay B does not judge individual cases, prepare or file documents on anyone's behalf, or charge fees for administrative work. Those services are provided only by qualified partner administrative agents, under their own name and responsibility. Job introductions are handled by registered employment agencies. Stay B does not provide matchmaking or marriage brokering.
